Find the worlds worth a second look.

Vespera trains a classifier on NASA's Kepler, K2 and TESS catalogs, scores every unconfirmed candidate in your table, and flags the ones that sit in the habitable zone.

Free to use. Nothing to install. Your file is processed on upload and the results are ready to download.

Three steps from a NASA table to a ranked list

  1. Download a mission table

    Export the Kepler, K2 or TESS candidate table from the NASA Exoplanet Archive as CSV, with "values only, no errors" selected. The archives are linked here.

  2. Pick the mission and a model

    Logistic regression, random forest, SVM or XGBoost. The model trains on the confirmed and false-positive rows of your own file, so it is always fitted to the data you brought.

  3. Read the results

    A CSV of candidate predictions, a second one with probabilities, a performance report, and a 3D map of every planet in parsecs around the Sun.

Three missions, one classifier

Each mission catalog has its own columns. Vespera knows which ones to read and what accuracy to expect on a held-out test split.

Mission Telescope Features used Test accuracy
Kepler Kepler, 2009–2013, one fixed field in Cygnus Orbital period, impact parameter, transit duration and depth, planet radius, equilibrium temperature, stellar log g and radius 90%
K2 Kepler, 2014–2018, fields along the ecliptic Orbital period, system distance, V, K and Gaia magnitudes 97%
TESS TESS, 2018 onward, nearly the whole sky Proper motion, transit duration and depth, planet radius, insolation, equilibrium temperature, TESS magnitude, distance, stellar temperature 85%

What counts as habitable here

A planet is flagged habitable when all four of these fall inside the window. The windows are deliberately conservative: roughly an Earth-sized world on a temperate orbit around a Sun-like star.
